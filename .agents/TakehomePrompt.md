The task: Design and build a one-page Student Dashboard — the home screen a student sees when they log in to manage a workflow of your choice. Pick any workflow you think a college student would want to better organize - registering for classes, job searching, applying to scholarships, etc.

Time: Plan for 1-2 hours. This isn't about how much you can cram in — it's about whether you can ship something that works and feels considered in the time given. We expect the dashboard to be visually clean, logically organized, and to include at least 2-3 functioning clickable interactions (not just links to nowhere). If you're tight on time, cut scope — fewer workflow states, a narrower feature set — rather than cutting quality on what you do include. Scope management is part of the job; we want to see you exercise it.

Format: Build it however you work fastest: Figma, HTML/CSS, v0, Framer, a no-code tool, whatever. Your submission must include real interactivity: at minimum, one primary action the student can complete on the dashboard itself (e.g., marking a task done, expanding a detail, filtering a list) — not just a link that goes nowhere. Anything the dashboard links out to can be a dead end; the dashboard itself should not be. A static image or single non-interactive screen will not meet the bar for this exercise, regardless of visual quality.

What the dashboard should help the student do: The student is mid-process on the workflow you choose. At a glance, your dashboard should help them understand:
What they need to do next
Where they stand / how far along they are
At least one thing they can act on directly from this screen
How you interpret and prioritize those is up to you - that's the point of the exercise. Add, cut, or reframe anything you think serves the student better.

What to submit:
The mock-up (link or file)
A short rationale — 5 bullets or a 2-minute Loom — covering:
What workflow you picked and why
What you chose to surface first, and why
One thing you deliberately left out or kept rough
What you'd build next with more time
How we'll evaluate it: We care most about your product judgment coupled with your ability to bring it to life. Did you build the right thing? Is it laid out in a way that makes sense for the student? Does it work — can we click through it and have it hold together? Visual craft matters, but it's secondary to clear thinking about what the student needs, how to prioritize it, and putting it in a format that showcases it.
